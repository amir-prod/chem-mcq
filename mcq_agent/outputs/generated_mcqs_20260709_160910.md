## Question 1

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** Boiling point depends mainly on molecular mass, so compounds with similar or lower mass cannot have much higher boiling points unless they are ionic.
- **Correct answer concept:** A substance capable of hydrogen bonding can have a substantially higher boiling point than a similar-mass substance that only exhibits dipole-dipole and London dispersion forces.
- **Expected reasoning:** Students should compare two or three molecular substances of comparable size, identify whether O-H, N-H, or F-H bonds enable intermolecular hydrogen bonding, and infer that stronger intermolecular attractions require more energy to separate molecules into the gas phase. Reasoning should explicitly weigh IMF type over small mass differences.
- **Style notes:** Use a ranking or pairwise comparison item such as ethanol vs dimethyl ether vs propane, avoiding memorized boiling-point values. Include one distractor that reflects the 'heavier always boils higher' idea. Ask for the best explanation, not just the correct order.

Ethanol, dimethyl ether, and propane have similar molar masses. Ethanol has a much higher boiling point than the other two. Which explanation best accounts for this difference?

- **A.** Ethanol molecules can form hydrogen bonds with each other, which are stronger than the intermolecular forces in dimethyl ether and propane. ✓
- **B.** Ethanol has the highest boiling point because any compound containing oxygen is ionic enough to behave like an ionic substance.
- **C.** Dimethyl ether should boil highest because it is slightly heavier than ethanol, and boiling point depends mainly on molar mass.
- **D.** Propane and dimethyl ether boil lower only because they have fewer covalent bonds than ethanol.

**Explanation:** Ethanol contains an O-H bond, so its molecules can hydrogen bond to each other. Dimethyl ether has polar bonds but no O-H, N-H, or F-H bond, so it cannot hydrogen bond to itself; propane has only London dispersion forces. Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to separate molecules, so ethanol boils much higher despite similar molar mass.

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** If a molecule contains polar bonds, it will necessarily have a higher boiling point than a nonpolar molecule of similar size, regardless of molecular shape or whether the dipoles cancel.
- **Correct answer concept:** Overall boiling point depends on the actual intermolecular attractions between whole molecules; a molecule with polar bonds may still be nonpolar overall if its geometry cancels dipoles, leaving London dispersion as the dominant IMF.
- **Expected reasoning:** Students should evaluate molecular geometry and symmetry, determine whether the molecule is overall polar, then connect molecular polarity to the type and relative strength of intermolecular forces present. They should conclude that a symmetric molecule with polar bonds may not have stronger dipole-dipole interactions than a comparable truly polar molecule.
- **Style notes:** Design around a comparison such as CO2 vs SO2 or CCl4 vs CHCl3, asking which has the higher boiling point and why. The stem should force students to consider whole-molecule polarity, not just the presence of polar bonds. Distractors should include 'contains polar bonds, so higher boiling point' and 'all covalent molecules only have dispersion forces'.

Carbon dioxide, CO2, and sulfur dioxide, SO2, are both molecular substances with polar bonds. SO2 has the higher boiling point. Which explanation best accounts for this?

- **A.** CO2 has the higher boiling point because its C=O bonds are more polar, so it must have stronger intermolecular forces.
- **B.** SO2 has the higher boiling point because it is polar overall, so its molecules experience dipole-dipole attractions in addition to London dispersion forces. ✓
- **C.** CO2 and SO2 should have the same boiling point because all covalent molecules only experience London dispersion forces.
- **D.** CO2 has the higher boiling point because its linear shape lets molecules pack farther apart, increasing attraction.

**Explanation:** Although both molecules contain polar bonds, CO2 is linear and its bond dipoles cancel, making the molecule nonpolar overall. SO2 is bent, so its dipoles do not cancel and it is polar. As a result, SO2 has dipole-dipole attractions in addition to London dispersion forces, giving it a higher boiling point.

## Question 3

**Status:** Approved  
**Revision rounds:** 0  
**Cognitive level:** analysis  

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** All molecules with the same molecular formula or molar mass have essentially the same boiling point because they have the same amount of electrons and therefore the same intermolecular forces.
- **Correct answer concept:** For isomers with the same formula, molecular shape affects contact surface area and thus the strength of London dispersion forces; less branched, more extended molecules usually have higher boiling points than more compact branched isomers.
- **Expected reasoning:** Students should recognize that London dispersion forces depend not just on electron count but also on how effectively molecules can approach and interact. They should infer that straight-chain isomers have greater surface contact and stronger cumulative attractions than highly branched isomers, leading to higher boiling points.
- **Style notes:** Use an isomer comparison or ranking, such as n-pentane, isopentane, and neopentane. Avoid requiring numerical data. The prompt should ask for the best explanation of the trend, with distractors tied to 'same formula means same boiling point' and 'more branching means stronger forces because atoms are closer together'.

The three isomers n-pentane, isopentane, and neopentane have the same molecular formula, C5H12, but different boiling points. Which statement best explains their trend?

- **A.** They should have nearly the same boiling point because they have the same formula and the same total number of electrons.
- **B.** Neopentane should have the highest boiling point because greater branching brings atoms closer together and strengthens intermolecular forces.
- **C.** n-Pentane has the highest boiling point because its less branched shape provides greater surface contact between molecules, strengthening London dispersion forces. ✓
- **D.** Isopentane has the highest boiling point because intermediate branching always produces the most balanced intermolecular attraction.

**Explanation:** All three isomers have the same formula, but their shapes differ. The more extended shape of n-pentane allows greater surface area contact between molecules, which strengthens London dispersion forces. More highly branched isomers are more compact and generally have weaker dispersion attractions, so they boil at lower temperatures.
