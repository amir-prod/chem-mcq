## Question 2

**Status:** Approved  
**Revision rounds:** 1  
**Cognitive level:** analysis  

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** Any molecule containing a polar bond automatically has a high boiling point, because bond polarity within a molecule is treated as equivalent to overall strong intermolecular attraction regardless of molecular geometry or net dipole.
- **Correct answer concept:** Overall molecular polarity, not just the existence of polar bonds, influences dipole-dipole forces; a symmetric molecule can have polar bonds yet be nonpolar overall and therefore may boil lower than an asymmetric isomer with a net dipole when other factors are similar.
- **Expected reasoning:** Students should inspect molecular geometry or structural symmetry to determine whether bond dipoles cancel. Then they should compare the strength of intermolecular attractions between molecules of similar size: the more polar molecule experiences stronger dipole-dipole attractions and thus the higher boiling point, assuming neither can hydrogen bond strongly. The analysis should explicitly separate intramolecular bond polarity from intermolecular force strength.
- **Style notes:** Use isomers or closely related compounds where one is more symmetric than the other. Provide enough structural information for dipole cancellation reasoning. Include distractors that select the molecule with the most polar bonds or the greatest number of electronegative atoms even if it is nonpolar overall.

Consider the two compounds below, neither of which can hydrogen-bond to itself: cis-1,2-dichloroethene and trans-1,2-dichloroethene. Which one should have the higher boiling point?

- **A.** cis-1,2-dichloroethene, because its bond dipoles do not cancel and it has a net molecular dipole ✓
- **B.** trans-1,2-dichloroethene, because it has the same polar bonds arranged more evenly
- **C.** trans-1,2-dichloroethene, because a more symmetric molecule always has stronger intermolecular forces
- **D.** They should have the same boiling point because both contain the same number of chlorine atoms

**Explanation:** Both molecules contain polar C-Cl bonds, but in the cis isomer the dipoles do not cancel, so the molecule is polar overall. That gives stronger dipole-dipole attractions than in the trans isomer, which is more symmetric and less polar overall.

## Question 3

**Status:** Approved  
**Revision rounds:** 0  
**Cognitive level:** application  

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** A student may believe that if a molecule can form hydrogen bonds, then adding more O–H groups does not substantially change boiling point because hydrogen bonding is treated as an all-or-nothing property rather than something that can increase the extent of intermolecular attraction.
- **Correct answer concept:** Among similarly sized molecules, a diol typically has a higher boiling point than an alcohol with one O–H group, because each molecule can participate in more intermolecular hydrogen bonding interactions, increasing the energy required to separate molecules into the gas phase.
- **Expected reasoning:** Students should compare molecules of similar carbon skeleton and recognize that both substances may hydrogen-bond, but the one with two hydroxyl groups can form a more extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonding network. They should conclude that stronger overall intermolecular attraction raises boiling point, not merely the presence or absence of hydrogen bonding. Reasoning should avoid reverting to molar mass alone and instead focus on the number and extent of hydrogen-bonding sites.
- **Style notes:** Use a direct comparison such as 1-propanol vs 1,2-propanediol or ethanol vs ethylene glycol. Keep molar masses reasonably close so the key discriminant is extent of hydrogen bonding. Ask which has the higher boiling point and why. Include distractors reflecting: (1) once a molecule can hydrogen-bond, extra O–H groups do not matter; (2) the slightly lower molar mass compound must boil lower regardless of IMF extent; (3) boiling point depends only on covalent bond strength within the molecule.

1-Propanol and 1,2-propanediol have similar three-carbon frameworks. Which substance is expected to have the higher boiling point, and what is the best reason?

- **A.** 1-Propanol, because both substances can hydrogen-bond, so having an extra O–H group does not significantly change boiling point.
- **B.** 1,2-Propanediol, because it can form a more extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonding network than 1-propanol. ✓
- **C.** 1-Propanol, because boiling point depends mainly on the strength of covalent bonds within each molecule.
- **D.** 1,2-Propanediol, because any compound with slightly greater molar mass must have the higher boiling point regardless of intermolecular forces.

**Explanation:** Both molecules can hydrogen-bond, but 1,2-propanediol has two hydroxyl groups, so each molecule can participate in more intermolecular hydrogen-bonding interactions. This increases the overall attraction between molecules and raises the energy required to boil the liquid.

## Rejected 1

**Reason:** MCQ quality failed  
**Revision rounds:** 3  

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** Nonpolar molecules have no meaningful intermolecular forces, so all nonpolar substances should have similarly low boiling points and branching or chain length should not matter.
- **Correct answer concept:** All molecules, including nonpolar ones, experience London dispersion forces; these become stronger with greater polarizability and more effective surface contact, so larger/less branched nonpolar molecules generally have higher boiling points than smaller/more branched ones.

Among the C5H12 isomers n-pentane, 2-methylbutane, and neopentane, which has the highest boiling point, and why?

- **A.** Neopentane, because greater branching makes the molecule bigger and raises its boiling point
- **B.** n-Pentane, because its less branched shape allows greater surface contact and stronger London dispersion forces ✓
- **C.** All three have the same boiling point because they have the same molar mass
- **D.** 2-Methylbutane, because nonpolar molecules have negligible intermolecular forces so shape does not matter

**Explanation:** All three substances are nonpolar, but all molecules experience London dispersion forces. For isomers with the same molar mass, the key difference is shape. The less branched n-pentane has a more extended shape, which allows more surface contact between molecules and therefore stronger dispersion forces. Stronger intermolecular forces require more energy to overcome, so n-pentane has the highest boiling point. More branching makes molecules more compact, which lowers surface contact and generally lowers boiling point among these isomers.

**Reason:** MCQ quality failed; duplicate: The candidate is a near-duplicate of Question 1. **Revision rounds:** 3  

### Blueprint

- **Target misconception:** Hydrogen bonding is thought to occur whenever a molecule contains hydrogen anywhere in its formula, so students may predict similarly high boiling points for compounds with O atoms even when no O–H, N–H, or F–H bond is present.
- **Correct answer concept:** A compound can have a much higher boiling point than a similarly sized comparison molecule if it can hydrogen-bond to itself; merely having an oxygen atom or being polar is not sufficient. Alcohols boil higher than comparable ethers because alcohol molecules form intermolecular O–H···O hydrogen bonds, whereas ethers cannot donate H-bonds to themselves.

Three compounds of comparable molar mass are ethanol, dimethyl ether, and propane. Which statement best explains why ethanol has the highest boiling point?

- **A.** Ethanol has the highest boiling point because its molecules can form hydrogen bonds with each other through an O–H bond. ✓
- **B.** Dimethyl ether has the highest boiling point because its oxygen atom lets its molecules hydrogen-bond to each other even without an O–H bond.
- **C.** Dimethyl ether has the highest boiling point because its polarity alone makes its intermolecular forces stronger than ethanol’s.
- **D.** Propane has the highest boiling point because its nonpolar molecules experience stronger attractions than the oxygen-containing compounds of similar mass.

**Explanation:** Ethanol contains an O–H bond, so ethanol molecules can both donate and accept hydrogen bonds to each other. Dimethyl ether contains oxygen and is polar, but it lacks an O–H, N–H, or F–H bond, so it cannot hydrogen-bond to itself in the same way. Propane is nonpolar and has only London dispersion forces. Therefore, ethanol has the strongest overall intermolecular forces and the highest boiling point.
